# Method-to-Product Principles

This is a product-focused summary of the method "קוד התת-מודע". It is not a replacement for the original method document and should not be treated as clinical or scientific validation.

## Product purpose

The method treats emotional and behavioral reactions as automatic patterns. A central progress measure is shortening the delay between pattern activation and conscious recognition.

HRV-C supports that goal by:

1. Learning a personal physiological baseline.
2. Detecting a sustained change rather than reacting to one noisy point.
3. Creating a calm invitation to notice what is happening.
4. Offering a short optional recognition flow.
5. Recording only user-confirmed context and usefulness.

## The app's role

The app is an external recognition aid. It is not a therapist and does not identify the pattern itself.

Correct product claim:

> A sustained change was detected relative to your personal range. This may be a useful moment to check what is happening for you.

Incorrect product claim:

> You entered a subconscious loop.

## Relevant method sequence

The method describes an automatic chain:

`Trigger → Beliefs → Interpretation → Thoughts → Emotion → Response`

The app has direct evidence only about sampled physiological data. It may invite the user to reflect on body, thought, emotion or context, but must not populate those answers for them.

## Three support layers

### 1. Exit a loop

Offer short support such as pausing, breathing, movement, changing environment or delaying a response.

### 2. Avoid entering a loop

This is the strongest HRV-C use case: provide an early recognition point before accumulated activation becomes overwhelming.

### 3. Long-term pattern change

The app may support reflection and history, but deep belief work remains outside automated inference. Never present the app as changing root beliefs by itself.

## Success metrics

- Reduced time between signal and self-recognition.
- More useful pauses before automatic reactions.
- User-confirmed usefulness of alerts.
- Fewer false or disruptive alerts.
- Increased independent recognition over time.

Alert count alone is not success.
